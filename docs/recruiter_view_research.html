Can a candidate know a recruiter opened their application?

Fourteen platforms, eight signals, and one uncomfortable finding: no applicant tracking system in the stack exposes a “viewed” event to anyone — not the candidate, not even the employer paying for it. Everything SpotApply could honestly show falls into three tiers, and only the first is proof.

Reliably knowable

Submission, delivery receipts, and opens of SpotApply’s own hosted links.

Estimable only

Whether a human — rather than a scanner — did the opening.

Structurally impossible

Recruiter activity inside Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby.

Competitive read

No incumbent does this. Two who tried are dead.

The column that matters

Column 8 is empty for all fourteen platforms. Confirmed Every “verification” product that exists — Greenhouse Real Talent with CLEAR, LinkedIn’s identity badges — runs in the opposite direction: the candidate proves their identity to the employer. Nowhere does a recruiter attest to a candidate in a way the candidate can see. If SpotApply wants “Recruiter verified,” it has to create the action, not detect it.

Two findings that survived adversarial re-checking

Both were re-verified against first-party documentation specifically to try to refute them, because both would change what gets built.

SmartRecruiters — sanctioned, but it is a sales problem, not an engineering one

The endpoint is real and its documented purpose is unusually explicit: retrieve a candidate’s application status and “expose it in your interface, so the candidate can see what status they are in for the job.” Confirmed That removes the policy risk entirely — which is rare and genuinely notable.

What it does not remove is the distribution problem. Partner credentials are issued case-by-case by SmartRecruiters’ Partner & API Team, and each employer must then separately authorize the integration over OAuth with an admin account. Status is returned only for applications the partner itself submitted through the Apply API, to an employer that has the API add-on enabled — there is no lookup-by-email path. So coverage grows one employer at a time, through a sales motion, not through shipping code. Treat it as an enterprise integration to keep on file, not as a route to broad coverage.

Greenhouse — the gap is real, and unreachable

MyGreenhouse Stages went GA in March 2026 on all product tiers, letting employers map internal pipeline stages to candidate-facing labels that update automatically as recruiters move people. Confirmed But it notifies nobody: “candidates will see their current stage in MyGreenhouse — no extra emails or manual updates required.” The only candidate email MyGreenhouse sends is new-job alerts. Checking status means a passwordless sign-in every time.

That is a genuine unmet need — a product that reliably said “your stage moved” would be doing something the ATS does not. It is also unreachable: there is no candidate-side Greenhouse API, the portal is the only surface, and reading it programmatically means automating a logged-in session. The gap persists precisely because there is no legitimate way to close it.

The decisive negative was checked directly against Greenhouse’s own API source repository rather than its documentation site: the Application object has no viewed field, the activity feed records only notes, emails and stage changes, and the webhook catalogue contains no view event. Greenhouse does not emit an “someone opened your application” signal to candidates, to employers, or to any API.

The honesty ladder

Seven statuses, three tiers of truth

These are genuinely ordered — each rung is a strictly stronger claim than the one below it, and the tier boundaries are where the evidence changes kind. Rungs 1–3 are facts SpotApply records. Rung 4 is an inference. Rungs 5–7 need someone else’s cooperation.

01

Submission confirmed

SpotApply’s extension observed a real submit click on the ATS page. Already implemented — content.js requires the literal word “submit”, rejects bare “apply”, checks isTrusted, and dedups, then posts to a state-guarded endpoint that stamps submitted_at. This is a fact about the candidate’s own browser and is fully reliable. It does not prove the ATS accepted the application.

02

Delivered

A confirmation email arrived from the ATS. Partly implemented — the extension already scrapes Gmail and Outlook and posts to /api/sync-emails, which flips applications to REJECTED and INTERVIEWING. Reliable when the email exists; the gap is that Lever, Ashby and Workday all make the confirmation email employer-optional, so absence means nothing.

03

Profile opened

A unique SpotApply-hosted link was loaded. This is the only rung where SpotApply owns the whole measurement, so it is the only engagement signal it can state as fact — but the fact is narrow: a request reached our server. It says nothing yet about who sent it.

04

Human engagement detected

Scroll depth, dwell time, or a click on an evidence item. This is where estimation begins and it cannot be promoted to fact: it is a likelihood that the opener was a person rather than a scanner. Achievable and worth building — but it must be labelled as an inference in the UI, every time.

05

Recruiter viewed

An authoritative platform said a recruiter viewed it. Available for exactly four platforms, only through the candidate’s own inbox, and never for an application submitted to Greenhouse, Lever, Workday or Ashby. SpotApply can relay this; it can never generate it.

06

Recruiter verified

A named person at a confirmed company domain took a deliberate, attributable action. No platform offers this, so SpotApply would have to create the action rather than detect it — which means it depends entirely on recruiter adoption, and is worth nothing until recruiters actually click it.

07

ATS stage changed

The employer moved the candidate. Reachable three ways, all partial: parsing rejection and interview emails (already built), the SmartRecruiters partner endpoint (narrow), and portal polling (barred by every portal’s terms). Genuinely useful, but coverage will always be patchy and stage labels mean different things at different employers.

The line that must not blur

Rungs 3 and 5 are the ones users will conflate, and the temptation to let them is the central product risk here. “Your profile was opened” and “a recruiter viewed your application” are different claims about different objects, and SpotApply can only ever prove the first. Any UI that lets the first read as the second is misleading, however carefully the tooltip is worded.

Prior art

Who already sells this, and what happened to them

Nobody in SpotApply’s category offers recruiter-view tracking. The products that offer something adjacent split cleanly into two groups: hosted-link analytics (which work, within their limits) and dedicated candidate-side engagement trackers (both of which died).

Hosted-link analytics — the working pattern

These prove the model is technically sound and commercially viable as a paid tier. All of them measure their own hosted page, which is precisely rung 3.

  • VisualCV Confirmed logs every view and download of a hosted résumé with date, IP, approximate location, view count and time spent; unique links per employer let the candidate infer which recruiter opened it. Paid tier.
  • resume.io Confirmed ships share-link statistics: views over time, viewer city/country, and duration per view.
  • Standard Resume Confirmed explicitly recommends one web résumé per application so you “know when the recruiter views your résumé.”
  • DocSend and the open-source Papermark Confirmed offer the richest version — per-visit identity via unique links, total time, and page-by-page dwell — but as general document tools, not job-search products.
  • LoopCV Confirmed shows which companies opened its auto-sent outreach emails. Email-pixel based, so it inherits every accuracy problem in the next section.
  • Mailtrack / Mailsuite Confirmed markets read receipts and PDF-open tracking directly at candidates wanting to know “whether your résumé has been opened by the recruiter.”
  • Kickresume Not offered hosts résumé websites but ships no view analytics.

Dedicated trackers — the cautionary pattern

Two companies built precisely the product being considered here. Both are gone.

  • Presumi (Sydney, 2015–2017) Reported sold trackable résumé links: opens, in-résumé link clicks, email delivery, plus a B2B analytics layer for job marketplaces. Last update April 2017; site dead by 2019.
  • StartWire Reported pushed automatic application-status updates by SMS and email, monitoring 20,000+ career pages plus ATS integrations — the closest historical analogue to inbox-parsing plus portal-scraping. Listed as permanently closed.

Neither death proves the idea is bad, but they establish the failure mode: a signal that depends on employer-side behaviour degrades as employers change their tooling, and a tracker with patchy coverage is hard to keep alive on its own.

The category SpotApply competes in

Teal, Huntr and Simplify — the three trackers users actually compare SpotApply against — show zero employer-side signals between them. Simplify auto-detects the submit click; Huntr infers “applied” from its own autofill and states in its docs that it “assumes these actions happened”; Teal requires a manual dropdown. Simplify users are still filing feature requests asking for basic email-based status detection, which SpotApply already ships. Confirmed

The trap in the obvious fix

“Require JavaScript execution” is the standard answer to pixel noise, and it does eliminate bare fetchers, most unfurl bots, and simple parsers. It does not eliminate modern detonation sandboxes, which execute JavaScript for 30–90 seconds per URL — longer than a real recruiter’s 7.4-second scan. A naive “dwell > 10 seconds means human” rule therefore inverts: it admits the sandbox and rejects the recruiter. Interaction shape — scroll events, page turns, pointer entropy — discriminates where duration alone does not. Note also that DocSend, the category leader in document-view tracking, publicly disclaims pixel-tier accuracy in its own marketing.

Direct answers

The nine questions

01Can SpotApply reliably know that a recruiter opened an external Workday, Greenhouse, Lever or Ashby application?

No, and not because of an access problem — because the event does not exist. None of these four systems records a candidate-visible open, view, or download. Greenhouse’s published webhook catalogue and Ashby’s 22 event types contain no view event; Lever and Ashby have no candidate portal at all. The employer paying for the ATS cannot see this either, except through Greenhouse’s separate employer-only audit-log add-on. There is nothing to intercept, buy, or negotiate for.

02Can SpotApply access employer-side ATS activity without the employer’s permission?

No. Every ATS in scope issues API credentials only to an employer administrator or to a vetted partner acting on an employer’s behalf. Candidate-side products have no credential path anywhere.

SmartRecruiters is the apparent exception and it proves the rule: it openly sanctions showing a candidate their status in a third party’s interface, but only after that partner is credentialed by SmartRecruiters and authorized by each employer individually. That is employer permission, obtained one employer at a time.

The two channels that remain both belong to the candidate rather than the employer: parsing their own inbox, and reading what their own portal login shows them. The first is clean. The second is barred by the portals’ own terms — Workday’s prohibit data mining and automated extraction, SmartRecruiters’ candidate terms bar access by automatic means, and Handshake’s bar scraping. Using the candidate’s own credentials does not cure that.

03Can a unique grounded-profile link show that someone opened it?

Yes — this is the one thing in the whole report SpotApply can measure directly. A unique per-recipient URL on SpotApply’s own domain gives an unambiguous server-side fact: this token was requested, at this time, from this IP, with this user agent and referer. VisualCV, resume.io, Standard Resume and DocSend all ship exactly this. What it establishes is that a request arrived — not who sent it, and not that they read anything.

04Can SpotApply prove the visitor was the recruiter rather than a bot?

No. It can raise confidence substantially, but proof is not available. Bot filtering by user agent and IP range removes the obvious crawlers; requiring JavaScript execution rather than a bare image load removes most scanners; requiring dwell time and scroll depth removes nearly all of the rest. What survives is still only a strong inference — a corporate security scanner that renders pages, or a colleague the recruiter forwarded the link to, both look like engaged humans. And the inverse error is permanent: a recruiter who reads the page in a reading-mode extension or with JS disabled registers as nothing.

05What explicit action would be required to call someone “Recruiter verified”?

All four of these, together:

  • A recruiter account whose mailbox ownership at a non-free corporate domain has been proven by a one-time code sent to that address — not merely a domain string typed into a form. SpotApply’s current _verify_recruiter() explicitly refuses to treat the typed version as proof, and its code comments name the hole. Closing it is a prerequisite, not a nice-to-have.
  • A deliberate, attributable click on a specific candidate — “I reviewed this” or “Request interview” — that the recruiter knows the candidate will see.
  • A server-recorded event the client cannot forge, tied to that recruiter’s authenticated session.
  • Wording that states what was actually attested: a named person at a confirmed domain took an action on a date. Not that they endorse the candidate, and not that they verified any résumé claim — no platform anywhere offers that, and SpotApply should not imply it either.

06Is email-open tracking accurate enough to show candidates?

No. Do not build a candidate-facing feature on email opens. The detail is in the next section, but the summary is that pixel-based opens now generate both false positives at scale (privacy proxies and security scanners fetching images the recipient never saw) and false negatives (blocked images), in proportions SpotApply cannot measure per-recipient. A candidate who follows up on a phantom open pays a real social cost. Tracked links on SpotApply’s own domain, with scanner filtering, are meaningfully better — that is rung 3, not rung 6.

07Are any competitors already offering this?

Not in this category, and the two who tried are dead. Résumé builders sell hosted-link view analytics (VisualCV, resume.io, Standard Resume) and general document tools do it better (DocSend, Papermark). But the trackers SpotApply competes with — Teal, Huntr, Simplify — show no employer-side signal whatsoever. Presumi and StartWire both built dedicated candidate-side engagement tracking and both shut down.

08Is this feature technically feasible without partnerships with employers?

Rungs 1–4 yes, entirely. Rung 6 yes but it depends on recruiter behaviour, not employer contracts. Rungs 5 and 7 only partially, and never for the four ATSs that matter most. The uncomfortable shape of this is that feasibility runs inversely to what candidates actually want: the signals SpotApply can build unilaterally are the ones about its own surfaces, and the signal candidates ask for — “did a recruiter at this company read my application” — is precisely the one that needs cooperation nobody will give.

09What can SpotApply honestly show without misleading users?

Four things, stated as what they are:

  • “You submitted this on [date].” A fact SpotApply observed.
  • “[Company]’s system confirmed receipt on [date].” A fact, quoted from their email.
  • “Your profile link was opened 3 times — most recently from a [Company] network.” A fact about SpotApply’s own server, with the inference about who clearly separated from the observation.
  • “LinkedIn says the employer viewed your application.” Attributed to LinkedIn, because the claim is theirs.

And two things it must refuse to show: a “recruiter viewed” badge derived from an email-open pixel, and any inference of interest drawn from silence. On four of the fourteen platforms, absence of a signal is the normal state for applications that were read carefully.

Verdict

What to build

Reliably knowable

  • That the candidate submitted, and when — observed in their own browser.
  • That the employer’s system acknowledged receipt, where a confirmation email exists.
  • That a SpotApply-hosted link was requested, by which network, at what time, with what referer.
  • Whatever LinkedIn, Indeed, ZipRecruiter or Handshake state in an email to the candidate — relayed with attribution, never re-badged as SpotApply’s own finding.
  • Rejections and interview invitations, from inbox parsing that already works.

Estimable only

  • Whether a human rather than a scanner opened the link. Good filtering gets this to a confident inference; it never becomes proof.
  • Which company the viewer came from — IP and referer suggest it, corporate VPNs and home networks blur it.
  • Whether attention was real. Dwell and scroll help, and the 7.4-second first-pass norm means a genuine read can look identical to a glance.
  • Whether a stage change reflects human judgement or an automation rule. Nothing in the data distinguishes them.

Impossible without employer cooperation

  • Any recruiter activity inside Greenhouse, Lever, Workday or Ashby. Not gated — nonexistent. The event is not recorded, so no amount of access would surface it.
  • Who specifically opened an application, on every platform in this report.
  • Whether a hiring manager, as distinct from anyone else on the team, saw it.
  • Any recruiter verification of a candidate’s claims. No platform offers the concept.

The smallest honest version worth building

A tracked evidence link attached to applications, plus relay of the platform signals already arriving in the candidate’s inbox. Concretely, and in this order:

  1. Per-application tracked link on SpotApply’s domain, opt-in and revocable, with a proper 128-bit token rather than today’s 16-bit handle.
  2. View logging with bot filtering from day one — user-agent classification, data-center ASN detection, a send-time discard window, and honeypot links. Not a follow-up ticket.
  3. An interaction beacon reporting scroll and dwell, surfaced as a separate and clearly weaker claim than the view itself.
  4. Extend the existing email sync to capture LinkedIn, Indeed and ZipRecruiter view notifications. Highest value per unit of work in this entire report — the parsing infrastructure exists and these are the only authoritative recruiter-view signals a candidate ever receives.
  5. Three-tier status vocabulary in the UI: Submitted → Delivered → Opened, with human engagement and platform-reported views as annotations on the third, never as separate stages implying progression.

The recruiter “Verify” button is a second phase. It needs a transactional email provider and signed tokens, neither of which exists in the codebase, and its value is entirely contingent on recruiters showing up — which the view-rate data from phase one would actually measure. The existing internal spec sets a sensible kill threshold: under 2% view rate at 90 days means recruiters are ignoring the link.

Is this a competitive advantage?

A real but narrow one, and not the one it looks like.

Against Teal, Huntr and Simplify the gap is genuine: none of them shows any employer-side signal, and SpotApply’s existing submit detection and inbox parsing already put it ahead. Extending to platform view-notification relay would make it the only tracker in that group surfacing authoritative recruiter views, and hosted-link analytics are demonstrably monetizable as a paid tier.

But the advantage is not defensible on technology — hosted-link analytics is a well-understood pattern that any competitor could copy in weeks. And the ceiling is low where it matters most: for applications to Greenhouse, Lever, Workday and Ashby — the ATSs behind most serious tech roles — SpotApply will show a tracked-link open and nothing else, forever. Two companies built exactly this product and neither survived.

The strategic read

Treat this as a trust and anti-ghosting feature, not a surveillance one. The durable version of the advantage is not “we can see recruiters” — that claim is mostly unbackable and invites a competitor to make the same claim more loudly and less carefully. It is that SpotApply tells candidates the truth about what is knowable, in a market where the honest answer to “did anyone read this?” is usually nobody can tell you. That position is hard to copy precisely because copying it means giving up the overclaim.

Method and confidence

How this was researched, and where to distrust it

Sourcing limitation — read before quoting any number

This research environment’s egress proxy blocked direct fetches of a large share of the primary domains: linkedin.com, learn.microsoft.com, support.indeed.com, ziprecruiter.com, joinhandshake.com, support.greenhouse.io, developers.smartrecruiters.com, workday.com, and Apple, Google and Litmus documentation.

Where a page could not be opened, its content was read through search-engine extraction of that same page. Claims marked Confirmed rest on first-party text obtained that way, and the wording is very likely verbatim but cannot be guaranteed against light paraphrase. Two exceptions were read directly and are the strongest evidence in the report: Greenhouse’s API documentation source repository on raw.githubusercontent.com, and Lever’s postings-api repository.

Practical implication: the structural findings are solid and consistent across many independent sources. The specific percentages in the noise-floor section should be re-verified from the primary pages before any of them appears in user-facing copy or a pitch deck.

Grade definitions
Confirmed
First-party documentation states it. Subject to the extraction caveat above.
Reported
A named secondary source states it, or first-party content that could not be corroborated. Directionally trustworthy, numerically soft.
Unknown
Checked and not resolved. Distinguished throughout from “confirmed absent”, which is a much stronger claim and is stated as such where it applies.
Known open items
  • Whether Indeed notifies candidates when an employer views their résumé in résumé search, as opposed to viewing an application. No first-party page was found stating either answer. Do not assert either direction.
  • Whether ZipRecruiter surfaces a résumé-download signal. Its candidate help centre documents view and “contact info looked at” alerts but no download state.
  • A conflicting figure in LinkedIn’s ATS documentation suggesting applicant email notifications arrive 72 hours after the action, against marketing copy describing real-time updates. If notification latency matters to the design, verify before relying on it.
  • Whether Handshake counts an employer opening an application as a profile view in its Profile Viewers list. Undocumented.
  • Whether a SmartRecruiters public, unauthenticated status endpoint exists. A reference slug appears in the search index but could not be read; the platform states unauthenticated access is limited to publicly available data, which would exclude application status. Treat as unlikely rather than ruled out — it is the one finding that would change the economics.
